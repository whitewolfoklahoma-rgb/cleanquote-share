How residential pricing works

First the app estimates the work in cleaner-hours (one cleaner, start to finish):

  • Base time = home sq ft ÷ your base production rate (default 450 sq ft/hr) + bedrooms × 10 min + bathrooms × 20 min.
  • Service factor — standard ×1.0, first-time ×1.4, deep ×1.6, move-in/out ×1.8. A deep clean simply takes longer, and the price follows the time.
  • Condition factor — light ×1.0, moderate ×1.2, heavy ×1.45.

Then it prices two ways — you pick per quote:

  • Per-hour: cleaner-hours × your hourly rate, plus add-ons.
  • Flat: your sq-ft tier price × service factor × condition factor, plus add-ons. The hours estimate still shows, so you can see whether the flat price actually pays for the time.

Crew size changes how long you'll be on site (cleaner-hours ÷ crew), never the price or the cost — two cleaners for half the time cost the same as one cleaner all day.

How commercial pricing works

Cleaner-hours = sq ft ÷ the production rate for the area type (office 2,500, retail 3,000, medical 1,800, restaurant 1,500 sq ft/hr by default — medical and food spaces are slower because of detail and sanitizing). Price = cleaner-hours × hourly rate × timing factor (nights ×1.15, weekends ×1.10, night + weekend ×1.25), then the frequency discount comes off.

Recurring value — the number that matters

Frequency discounts are the price of loyalty: weekly −15%, biweekly −10%, monthly −5% by default (editable). In exchange, the quote shows what the client is worth: weekly = 52 visits a year, biweekly = 26, monthly = 12. A $95 weekly visit is a $4,940-a-year client. That framing — per-visit price vs. annual value — is how you decide which clients deserve the discount and which ones to protect with your floor.

The profit floor

Every quote is costed honestly: labor (cleaner-hours × crew pay) + supplies (% of labor) + travel (round-trip miles × vehicle cost), marked up by your overhead % to get break-even. Your floor is the lowest price that still leaves your minimum margin (default 30%) — and never less than your minimum job charge. If you type a final price below the floor, CleanQuote flags it red and will not save the quote unless you tick the one-time override box and own the decision. Loading a saved quote to edit it, saving, or raising the price back above the floor resets the override — you can never override by accident or by habit.

Localize the defaults — important

Every rate in this app is a national planning default: $55/hr charged, $22/hr crew pay, 450 sq ft/hr base production, the tier table, the add-on prices. They are starting points, not your market. Before quoting real work: time your next three cleans and compare them with the estimate, check what local competitors charge, and set your numbers in Settings. A calculator with wrong inputs gives confidently wrong prices — this one makes yours easy to fix.

Quote statuses & follow-up

New quotes start as Draft. Mark them Sent when they go out — the Follow-up list then tracks every sent-but-unanswered quote oldest-first, so nothing quietly dies. Accepted = the client said yes; Booked = it's on the schedule; Declined = they passed. Booked value in the Quote Book counts booked quotes at their per-visit price plus their recurring value where it applies.

Your data

Everything is stored only in this browser (localStorage) on this device — no account, no cloud, no tracking, works fully offline. Use Export backup in the Quote Book before switching browsers or devices. Clearing browser data erases the app — export first.
